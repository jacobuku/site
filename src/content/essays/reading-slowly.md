I used to track how many books I finished each year. The number went up. My memory of them did not.

The problem with reading fast is not that you miss things — you always miss things. The problem is that speed changes what you are doing. You stop arguing with the book. You stop putting it down to stare out the window for ten minutes. Those interruptions were not wasted time; they were the part where the reading actually happened.

## What I do now

I read one book at a time, and I let it take as long as it takes. Some take a month. A few I abandon at page forty, which is its own kind of progress.

When a sentence stops me, I copy it out by hand. Not into a system with tags and backlinks — just a notebook I never reread. The copying is the point. It forces the sentence through at the speed of a pen instead of the speed of an eye.

## The uncomfortable part

Reading slowly means reading less. There is no trick that gets you both.

I have made peace with this by admitting that the alternative was never really working. A hundred books I cannot describe are not more than twelve I can. The count was measuring the wrong thing, and it was easy to measure, which is exactly why I kept measuring it.
